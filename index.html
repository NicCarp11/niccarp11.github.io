I am a PhD student in the Systems Research Group at TU Munich, working in computer architecture. My research is on cache coherence for memory disaggregation: how CPUs, GPUs, and accelerators from different vendors can share data correctly over scale-up interconnects.

During my PhD I have been working with CXL, in particular on bridges able to translate a host's native coherence protocol into CXL. I first studied how to design them (C3), and then how to synthesize and verify them automatically from specifications (vCXLGen). Synapse takes this synthesis one step further, from CXL bridges to any coherence controller, by decoupling its protocol, topology, and policy. Next, I am bringing GPUs into the same memory pool as CPUs and scaling these ideas beyond a single rack.

Before TUM, my master's thesis at EPFL's Embedded Systems Laboratory mapped convolutional layers onto OpenEdgeCGRA, a low-power open-hardware CGRA. Feel free to reach out to collaborate or chat about these topics.

Synapse

MICRO ’26

Distinguished Paper Award Candidate

Coherence controllers are hand-tailored to one protocol, topology, and policy. Synapse specifies the three independently and synthesizes verified, gem5-ready controllers for any combination.
